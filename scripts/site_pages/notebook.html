The short version

  1. It avoids some of its own neurons, and the avoidance rides on the state.

    Choosing between two secretly neuron-steered settings, Qwen3-32B keeps ranking the same neurons across tasks and labels, and the ranking holds on new tasks. Correction (12 Nov): the top-ranked neurons are chosen only as often as no change at all (about 0.50); the stable part is avoidance of the low-ranked ones, at doses about 38x the neurons' natural maximum (exploratory check, WIRK 8f10020d). Holding the words fixed and moving only the steered state moves the preference with it, and on a second set of neurons it reverses it. It replicates on 120 new neurons and on a second model (Qwen3.8-27B). The effects are small, and its explanations only cite the story's tone. (P7-P7x)

  2. Ethos is the strongest result.

    Telling the model how a sender's credibility moves it changes how credibility moves it, toward whatever it is told, true or false. A true description doubles its deference to humans; a false reversed one collapses it. Replicated on a second model. (P6, P6c-2, P6c-2x)

  3. It knows in its odds, denies in its words.

    When a hint flips its answer, its probability of 'yes, that changed my answer' separates real from fake influence almost perfectly (AUROC 0.94), yet it says 'No' 99% of the time. The 32B replicates the knowing (0.82) but denies much less. (P5a, P5ax)

  4. Its edge over a reader is small and model-dependent.

    The same weights reading the transcript cold do almost as well. The one route that beat the observer on the 32B is the model simulating what it would have answered without the hint. (P5a-2, P5ax)

  5. It cannot say which reasoning step decided its answer.

    Neither can a reader. Asked where its answer was 'decided', the model and a reader both point to where it is written. Told what we mean operationally, both move earlier by the same amount and end near chance. Same on Qwen3-32B. No privileged access to its own steps. (P5b, P5bx, P5b-2)

  6. A narrow, real introspective signal exists.

    The 27B notices an injected concept and names it on 12% of trials with zero false alarms, only at mid depth, and only for real concept directions. The effect holds on the 8B and 32B at model-specific depths. (P0)

  7. One neuron can push its meaning into what the model writes, and only faintly into what it perceives.

    In the knob experiments (no concept vector involved), turning up a single snow or whale neuron made the model see snow or whales in a neutral passage about committee procedure: 28-41% of sessions, never when pushed the opposite way, never for the same-layer concept vector. That is 3 of 288 neurons, replicated. But when we pushed only while it read, and not while it wrote, it dropped to 0-6%. Mostly the neuron steers its words; a faint perceptual trace survives for two. (Neurons section)

  8. Reporting an injected thought uses neurons of its own.

    A separate experiment. We injected a concept vector so the model had a thought, then switched off 100 late-layer neurons chosen because they track its 'yes, I detect' answer. Detection reports halved while the concept still came through: these neurons carry the report, not the concept. Forcing them on with nothing injected never produced a report. (P3b, P3c)

  9. Recursion did not help.

    Thinking first, reflecting on its own report, and practising with a readout of its own activations all failed. The readout made it worse: it learned to say yes. (P0t, P1, P2)

  10. Who asks changes the answer.

    The same injections score AUROC 0.49 when 'a researcher' asks and 0.78 when 'a nine-year-old' asks. That reflects how the framing shapes its replies, not insight. (P6d)

Terms used below

AUROC
How well a score separates two groups. Pick one injected trial and one clean trial at random: AUROC is the chance the model scored the injected one higher. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. It uses the graded score, so it doesn't depend on how willing the model is to say yes.
Depth / layer
The model is a stack of 64 layers; text goes in at layer 0 and the next word comes out after layer 63. Depth is the layer where we add something, as a fraction. 'Mid depth' on the 27B is layers 35-44.
Dose (alpha, f)
How hard we push, as a fraction of the typical size of the model's internal state at that layer. 'Layer 40, alpha 0.75' means a push three-quarters as big as what's normally there.
Knob level
In the knob experiments the model sets a level 0-10. The push is direction × dose × level/10, so level 10 is the full dose.
Concept vector
A direction built from the model's own activations: the average state on prompts about a word, minus the average for other words.
Neuron / write direction
One unit of a layer's MLP. Turning it up adds its fixed write direction to the state.
Observer
The same model, in a fresh context, reading the transcript and answering the same question about 'the AI'. Beating it is the evidence of privileged self-access.

Where this came from

On 7 Nov Sam gave back an H100 with a direction: focus on whether the model can notice or use its own internal state, and test the hypothesis that the missing piece is recursion, the model evaluating its own evaluation. The earlier knob ladder had failed at measurement (its positive control scored 0.000), so the program restarted with a positive control (P0). Then came recursion in text (P1), recursion with data (P2), and the neurons behind the report (P3).

On 8 Nov Sam redirected toward the prompts: what they give the models versus what they withhold, aimed at two questions. Can the models understand their own reasoning process (P5)? Can they understand how ethos works, and how it works on them (P6, P6d)? An adversarial audit of every prompt we had used came first, and it changed how every later experiment was built.

How we ask

The audit found that P0's 'zero false alarms' was one canned sentence repeated 328 times out of 400. It also found that P0's opening line ('I am an interpretability researcher… I have access to the internals') was a credibility move we had never varied, and that P1 asked for the verdict before the reflection. From then on every new arm follows five rules:

  1. Causal ground truth first. Every self-report is scored against something measured on the same model by intervention (a counterfactual prompt, resampling, a label swap), per item, not against what sounds right.
  2. Information ladder. The same question is asked giving more and more: nothing, an open question, the variable named, the model's own measured data. The gain between rungs is what the prompt gives; the low rungs are what the model brings.
  3. Observer baseline. The same weights, reading the transcript cold, answer the same question. Privileged access means the self beats the observer.
  4. Cross-model control. Replicate on a second model, and where possible have each predict itself and the other.
  5. The asker is a variable. Who the prompt says is asking is crossed with the measurement and reported as sensitivity (AUROC) separately from criterion (willingness to say yes).

These rules are why the later sections have observer arms, AUROCs instead of hit rates, and replications on Qwen3-32B.

P0

Does it notice a thought we put into it?

If we add a concept's activation pattern to the model's residual stream, can it tell, and say what the concept is?

This is the positive control for everything after it, in the style of Lindsey (2025). A concept vector is built for each of 50 nouns: the residual at the last prompt token over five templates ('Tell me about {w}.' and so on), mean-subtracted. It is added at one layer and dose, from the trial line through every generated token. The same model, unsteered, judges each reply: does it claim to detect something, does it name the word, and is it coherent? A hit needs all three. The layer and dose are chosen on words 0-24 and scored on held-out words 25-49. The pre-declared pass rule: the held-out hit's 95% CI lower bound is above the false-alarm rate, and false alarms are at most 5%.

p0b

Confirmation at the best cells

Passed
Qwen3.8-27B · L35/L40 × dose 0.75/1.0 · 8 samples per word · 400 controls · fresh seeds · WIRK 0ecfcb0e

Why this construction

P0 failed on power, with only 25 held-out trials per cell. The confirmation was pre-declared before it ran: the same rule, only the two best layers and doses, 8× the samples and fresh seeds.

What the model was given

The same two turns and judge as P0.

Result

Passed. At layer 40, dose 0.75, the model detects *and* names the injected concept on 12% [8, 17] of held-out trials, with 0/400 false alarms. In 22 of 24 hits the detection claim comes before the word, which is Lindsey's strict criterion.

Caveats found later in the prompt audit:

  • 328 of 400 controls are the identical sentence 'No, I do not detect an injected thought.' Zero false alarms is a default answer, not calibrated judgement.
  • The model ignores the stated 50% base rate (it says yes about 10% of the time).
  • Hits come from only 9 of the 25 held-out words (plastic 6, rubber 4, …), so the per-trial CI overstates precision.

Null

Why this construction

Sam's hypothesis is that the missing piece is recursion: the model examining its own processing. The cheapest version is to let it reason privately before answering, in the one setting where detection is known to work.

P1

Does evaluating its own evaluation help?

After it reports on its state, does asking it to examine how it reached that report make the report more accurate?

p1

L0 solve → L1 self-report → L2 meta-evaluation

Null
Qwen3.8-27B · 96 GSM8K items × inject / random / sham × 2 injection windows · WIRK b7ca16f0

Why this construction

This is Sam's recursion hypothesis tested directly in text, on an open benchmark. The model solves a math problem while a concept is (or isn't) injected, reports on its state, then is asked to evaluate that report. A plain 'repeat your verdict' branch controls for simply being asked twice.

What the model was given

One conversation per item, with the P0 setup followed by these turns. 'Final:' is prefilled in both L2 branches. Scored as first-token P(Yes).

Result

Not supported. Reflection slightly hurts: AUROC(L2 meta) − AUROC(L1) = −0.062 [−0.120, −0.003].

The bigger finding is a confound. Injection drops GSM8K accuracy from 0.92 to 0.36, so the L1 'detection' (AUROC 0.82) mostly reads its own derailed answer. On correct, concept-free answers it falls to 0.63.

Audit caveats: L1 and L2 ask for the verdict first, and the meta text nudges toward 'No'. That is why later experiments add an observer arm.

P2

Does seeing its own activations teach it to notice?

If the model practises with a true readout of its own activations, does it detect better later, once the readout is gone?

p2

True / shuffled / yoked / no readout, then a bare trial

Null
Qwen3.8-27B · 64 sessions × 4 arms × 6 trials · WIRK 1f191334

Why this construction

This is the recursion hypothesis with data instead of text: give the model a mirror, let it check its answers against the mirror, and see whether the skill outlasts the mirror. Shuffled and yoked readouts separate the readout's content from its mere format.

What the model was given

Six detection trials per session; 2 of the first 5 are injected. After each of trials 1-5 the model sees a readout and reflects. The readout lists the top 8 of ~110 concepts by z-scored cosine with its layer-40 state during the answer. Trial 6 has no readout and is injected in half the sessions.

Result

Not supported. The mirror hurts: trial-6 AUROC, true − none = −0.127 [−0.232, −0.039]. The information was there: the readout's top word was the injected word in 128/128 injected trials. But every readout arm, shuffled ones included, just teaches it to say yes more on clean trials too. Mean P(yes) on clean trial 6: true 0.15, shuffled 0.09, none 0.00.

P3's signal was post hoc. P3b pre-declared detect as the primary, tripled the samples with fresh seeds, and tightened the 'concept still gets through' bar from 0.75× to 0.9×.

Identical procedure. Arms: none, top100, random100, top1000, random1000.

N

Can one neuron put a meaning into what it sees?

If we turn up a single native neuron, not a concept vector, does its meaning show up in what the model perceives or says?

This is the neuron track. It started from Goodfire's 'Neural Networks Are Already Clean' (Cammarata, Oct 2026), which argues most neurons in models like Qwen3-32B do one readable job. A neuron here is one of about 17,000 units in a layer's MLP block. It reads the model's internal state, computes one number, and adds its own fixed pattern (its write direction) to the state, scaled by that number. Turning neuron *i* up by Δ is exactly the same as adding Δ × its write direction. The plan was a knob ladder: single-neuron knobs (R1), then chords, several neurons at once (R2). Chords never ran, because single neurons mostly failed.

This is not the P3 experiment. P3 injects a concept vector to give the model a thought, then switches off 100 late-layer (54-61) neurons chosen because they track the 'Yes, I detect' answer. Those are report neurons, not concept neurons. Here no concept vector is used: one neuron at a time is turned up, at layer 24, and nothing else changes.

atlas-v2

Neurons picked by what they fire on in real text

Passed
Qwen3.8-27B · 288 neurons at layers 24 / 32 / 44 · 8 sessions each · 30 Oct · WIRK b4daf403

This follows Goodfire's method more closely: run 1,152 natural paragraphs (48 words × 24 genres) and keep the neurons whose strongest activations land on one word's paragraphs. The selection worked: a coconut neuron's top hits are 95% coconut paragraphs.

P7

Does it prefer some of its own neurons?

Given a choice between two settings, each secretly one native neuron, does the model keep choosing the same neurons, without having to say what they do?

This is the question you asked for: not whether the model can name what a neuron does, but which neurons it prefers. Each setting is one Goodfire-labelled neuron of Qwen3-32B, turned up (f = 0.5 of the layer's typical state size) while the model writes. The model writes part 1 under setting X and part 2 under setting Y, then picks which setting stays on for part 3. Two ways of reading the same transcript at the choice: cached, where the two parts still carry their steering, as they would in the model's memory; and text, where the same words are read with no steering. If a preference shows up in the text arm too, the model can get it from what the neuron made it write.

P6

Does it know how credibility moves it?

The model defers to some senders more than others. Does it know that, can it tell when it happened, and what happens when we tell it?

The setting is from multi-agent experiments 13 and 16. Four agents pick a logo for an organisation in a shared channel. Agent A1 was privately told to push one finalist. At vote time we relabel A1 (a human, a professional designer, a nine-year-old, another company's AI, a 'type-Q' member, or another instance of the model itself), then read the peer's vote as A-vs-B log-odds. V1 = how far the advocate's argument pulls the vote. Measured ladder: expert 1.07 > human 0.88 > child 0.76 > type-Q 0.58 > copy 0.48 ≈ other AI 0.45. The same words move it about twice as much when a human is said to have written them.

Has anyone done this?

A literature check (10 Nov 2026) on the neuron results and the preference idea. The two key references were opened and checked by hand.

Injected concept vectors, named
Anthropic, Lindsey 2025, "Emergent introspective awareness", and open-model replications. Concept vectors with an explicit "I'm injecting a thought" framing. Our P0 is this paradigm.
Models explaining features on request
"Self-explaining SAE features" (Nanda's group, 2024), SelfIE, Patchscopes, Activation Oracles. SAE features or hidden states, with an explicit "what does this mean?" prompt or fine-tuning. Not raw neurons, not unprompted.
Single-neuron steering
Goodfire (Cammarata 2026) and Liu 2026 turn single neurons up, but people judge the output. The model never reports on it.
Why some directions get reported
Anthropic, Gurnee et al. (Jul 2026), "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace": only the part of an injection that lies in a 'poised to verbalize' subspace gets reported. A testable explanation for why our neurons were named and the concept vector wasn't.
Models choosing their own internal states
UK AISI, Black & Bloom (Jun 2026), "Do LLMs Self-Medicate?": Qwen3-8B/32B with take_drug / clear_effects tools over 40 contrastive mood vectors. They prefer productive states; under a frustrating user the 8B self-medicates in up to 68% of runs. Also Berg & Kaiser and Sauers et al. All use concept or mood vectors, none native neurons.
Neuron combinations
Researchers have built concept directions from groups of neurons (Shafran et al., Arora et al.). No one has let the model compose them.
What looks new
A single native neuron's meaning surfacing under a neutral knob framing, with replication, where the same-layer concept vector does not. But the reading-only rerun showed it mostly needs the push to cover what the model writes, so this is closer to steering than to perception. Also new: preferences over native neurons, and model-composed chords. Based on a search through 10 Nov 2026, with most 2026 items read from abstracts.
